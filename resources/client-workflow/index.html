BUSINESS SYSTEMS

Plan a client workflow people can follow

Connect the inquiry, proposal, progress updates, and payment steps into one understandable journey.

By Bay Area Apps · Project planning guide

Map the first request

Start with the information needed to decide whether you can help. A name, reply address, project type, and description are often more useful than a long form full of questions the customer cannot answer yet. Distinguish required details from optional ones, and explain what will happen after submission.

Confirm the request was received

A customer needs to know whether a submission actually reached the business. Show a clear confirmation only after the request is saved. A reference number helps with follow-up. If the customer will use a private project page, explain how to keep and reopen it. Do not promise an email or text notification unless that notification is actually connected.

Make statuses mean something

Choose a small number of useful states such as received, under review, proposal ready, in progress, and completed. Each should describe a real stage. “Confirmed” should not simply mean a form was submitted. Where appropriate, explain the next action and who is responsible for it.

Keep proposals and payments connected

A payment request should relate to an agreed scope or milestone. Show what the amount covers and the relevant invoice or request. Payment status and project progress are separate: a customer can pay a deposit while development has not begun, or finish a review while a balance remains due. Opening a payment page is not proof that money arrived.

Protect the private part

Public project examples and private client records serve different purposes. Keep proposals, contact information, conversations, and payment details behind the correct access controls. Limit the information exposed in notifications and links. A private access link should be treated like a key, and shared only with the people who should see the project.

Build for the moments that fail

Consider duplicate submissions, interrupted connections, a changed proposal, or a lost access link. A customer should be able to retry safely and get a useful explanation. The business needs a way to verify identity and recover access. These details are part of the workflow, not finishing touches after the page looks good.
