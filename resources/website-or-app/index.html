DECISIONS

Does your business need a website or an app?

Choose the format around what customers need to do, how often they return, and what you need to maintain.

By Bay Area Apps · Project planning guide

Begin with the customer’s route

Think about how someone finds your business. A new customer arriving from a shared link or a search result usually needs an easy way to understand your service and take action. A website can explain the offer and accept an inquiry without a download. A frequently returning user may benefit from an app when the experience has a clear repeated purpose.

When a website is a useful first step

A service description, portfolio, request form, and public resource library fit naturally on a website. For example, a catering business can show its service options and collect guest count, event date, and menu preferences. The website does not need to solve every internal business task on day one. It should make the customer’s next step reliable.

When an app has a clear job

An app is more compelling when people use it repeatedly or need a focused experience suited to their device. Examples might include a daily work tool, a personal organizer, or a business workspace used throughout the day. Ask what the app offers beyond opening a page. The answer should be a useful experience rather than the presence of an app icon alone.

A website and app can work together

The public website can introduce the business and collect requests while a private app supports the owner’s daily work. Both can use the same underlying records. This avoids asking customers to download an app just to send a question, while giving the operator a focused way to manage the work. Shared data still requires proper access controls and error handling.

Plan for maintenance

Both formats need upkeep. Content changes, broken links, service integrations, and changing platform requirements need an owner. Consider who will update business details and how an issue will be reported. A project proposal should explain what maintenance is included, what costs are ongoing, and what happens after the initial handoff.

Make a decision you can test

Write the main customer journey and compare the smallest workable version in each format. Choose the one that reaches the audience and completes the task with the least unnecessary friction. If the choice remains unclear, a screen prototype or a focused website can help validate the workflow before a larger app commitment.
