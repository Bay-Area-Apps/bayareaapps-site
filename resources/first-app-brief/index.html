PLANNING

What to put in your first app brief

Turn an idea into a useful starting point without writing a technical specification.

By Bay Area Apps · Project planning guide

Put this guide into practice. Use the free app brief builder to prepare a draft you can copy or download. Your answers stay in that tool’s tab until you choose to share them.

Start with one person and one task

An app idea becomes easier to build when you can describe who opens it and what they are trying to accomplish. “An app for my business” is a starting point. “A customer can request a service and see whether it has been confirmed” gives the project a specific job. Write one sentence in that form before listing features.

Describe what happens today

Explain the current process, including the inconvenient parts. Perhaps requests arrive in several inboxes, important details are missing, or a customer has to ask for the same update twice. The point is to identify work the app should simplify. If the current process is already reliable, explain what a digital version should improve.

Choose the first useful version

Make two short lists: essential for the first version, and useful later. A service request might need contact details, the requested service, a status, and a reply. Loyalty points, chatbots, maps, and multiple staff roles may be later additions. A smaller first version still needs to complete its main task from beginning to end.

Write a sample journey

Describe the experience in plain language: the customer enters their request, sees confirmation that it was saved, opens a private page, and receives the business’s response. Include what should happen when something goes wrong. If the network is unavailable, should the app keep a draft? If a request is declined, what should the customer see? These details help define a usable product.

Include access, content, and payments

Say who needs to sign in, what information is private, and who is allowed to change it. Note any existing website, brand assets, or business service that must be connected. If payment is involved, explain when it should happen and what the customer is paying for. Do not include passwords or banking details in a project inquiry.

Bring a realistic starting brief

A useful first inquiry includes your audience, main task, essential features, existing website, budget range, and preferred timing. “Not sure yet” is a reasonable answer. You can also link to an experience you like and explain the part that works for you. The goal is a shared understanding, followed by a written proposal that makes the build and its limits clear.
