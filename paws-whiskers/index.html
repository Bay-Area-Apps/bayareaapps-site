Help understanding your next step

Worried about your pet? Let’s make your concerns clear.

When your request is reviewed, I can discuss the changes you describe, help you recognize general warning signs, and advise you to contact a veterinarian or emergency hospital when those concerns need veterinary attention. This is educational guidance and referral support from a veterinary assistant.

Recognize urgency

Learn which warning signs call for prompt veterinary attention, including signs an owner may not realize are serious. A message or photo cannot rule out an emergency.

Explain what you are seeing

Organize when the concern started, whether it is worsening, and changes in breathing, appetite, energy, vomiting, stools, or urination. Share relevant history and current veterinary instructions.

Prepare to reach a veterinarian

Get help summarizing your concerns for the clinic, gathering existing records, and preparing questions. Recovery support follows your veterinarian’s existing discharge instructions.

Examples of questions you can ask

  • “What information should I give the clinic about this change?”
  • “Which warning signs mean I should contact an emergency hospital?”
  • “Can you help me organize my veterinarian’s discharge instructions?”
  • “What should I ask my veterinarian at the appointment?”

Possible emergency? Contact an emergency veterinarian now.

Breathing difficulty, collapse or unresponsiveness, ongoing or repeated seizures, uncontrolled bleeding, major injury, or sudden abdominal swelling are reasons to seek immediate veterinary help. This list is not exhaustive.

This Help Line is not continuously monitored and does not provide an immediate emergency response. Do not wait for a message, phone appointment, approval, or payment before seeking veterinary care. If you are unsure about urgency, call your veterinarian or an emergency hospital directly. Neither a missing reply nor a symptom missing from this list means it is safe to wait.

Read AAHA’s pet emergency warning signs

Guidance does not diagnose a condition, prescribe treatment, or replace a veterinary examination. Phone support is arranged after availability is confirmed.
