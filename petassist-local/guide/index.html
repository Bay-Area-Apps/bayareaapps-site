A little preparation.
A more comfortable visit.

Online visit requests and private visit pages are temporarily unavailable. This page keeps the preparation notes so an existing visit, or a future one, is easier to organize.

For a new pet-care question, use Paws & Whiskers Help Line. For an existing visit, email info@bayareaapps.com. Do not send a gate code or private visit link in a public place.

Your visit checklist

  1. Check what is actually confirmed

    A requested date is a preference until the appointment is confirmed. Review the confirmed time, service, and price on the message you already have. Send changes by email while the website is paused, and keep them with that visit rather than starting a second request.

    If more than one pet is involved, name each pet and the task you want. Do not assume another pet is included in the first pet’s appointment or price.

  2. Make arriving straightforward

    The correct address, unit number, parking, and any gate or entry instructions belong in a private message, not on this public page. Mention stairs, an elevator, or anything else that affects arrival. An adult who can provide access should be present.

    An “on the way” update, when the service is open, is an appointment status. It is not a live map or an exact arrival time. Ask for a timing update in the visit conversation.

  3. Choose a comfortable space

    A quiet, well-lit area with room to move is enough. Keep the entrance clear and secure exterior doors and gates. If several animals live in the home, separate spaces can make arrival calmer.

    Keep a familiar towel or blanket nearby if your pet finds it comforting. Ask which supplies the visit needs and which are provided. There is no need to buy special equipment before the service is confirmed.

  4. Say what helps your pet

    Share how your pet usually reacts to visitors or handling: where they rest, whether they hide, known sensitivities, or a history of biting or scratching. Those details decide whether the requested task is a suitable fit. They are not a diagnosis.

    If something has changed since you booked, send an update describing what you observed. If your pet needs urgent care, contact a veterinarian or emergency hospital. Do not wait for a home visit or an email reply.

  5. Gather what you want to discuss

    Write your main questions and your pet’s regular routine. If the visit relates to care a veterinarian already directed, have those written instructions available. After surgery or an emergency stay, keep the discharge papers and the clinic’s phone number together.

    A home visit does not replace a veterinarian’s instructions or follow-up. Questions about a treatment plan belong with the clinic that wrote it.

  6. Keep private links private

    A private visit link shows appointment details and messages. Share it only with someone who should have that access. Do not post it on social media or in a review.

    While booking is paused, do not start a new checkout. If you already have a payment question, email with your order or visit reference and do not send card numbers.

Questions that belong with a visit

These are visit questions, not a separate Help Line request:

  • What should I have ready for the service we already discussed?
  • There are two pets at home. Can you confirm the time and total for both?
  • My pet is nervous around visitors. What should I tell you before arrival?
  • Do you have the parking and entry instructions?
  • Where can I find the confirmed time or payment status?

For a separate general question about feeding, grooming, behavior, or preparing for a veterinary visit, start at the Help Line and read the support preparation guide.

Before you close this page

Know whether you are asking about an existing visit or a new pet-care question. Those are different conversations. Urgent medical concerns go to a veterinarian or emergency hospital.
